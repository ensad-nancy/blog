# Look at Jazz — Why This Book Really Got to Me

In this post I’m reflecting on the article “Look at Jazz: Studio Helmo’s experimental poster designs for Jazzdor festival have been compiled into a book” by Ellis Tree for It’s Nice That (26 Nov 2025). It introduces a new book Look at Jazz that collects 56 experimental poster designs Studio Helmo made for the Jazzdor festival between 2019 and 2024 — a series that builds on over 20 years of collaboration between the designers and the festival. 

I’ll admit it: I’m a huge fan of Studio Helmo. Their work feels like a visual interpretation of improvisation itself — playful, unpredictable, and full of energy. Instead of just presenting dates and venues, these posters capture mood and rhythm, almost like design improvised in real time. 

What Ellis Tree highlights in the article — how Helmo uses everything from typography to photography to express different musical feels — really resonates with me. It reminds me why I got into design in the first place: to create meaning, not just relay information. 

At the same time, it makes me think about context. This kind of experimental visual language works beautifully for something as eclectic as a jazz festival. But could we bring this freedom into more everyday design work? That’s a challenge I keep coming back to in my own practice. 

In short, Look at Jazz isn’t just a pretty collection — for me, it’s a reminder that great design can feel alive, expressive, and emotionally open, not just functional. And that’s exactly why I love Helmo’s approach.
