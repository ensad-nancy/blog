# First Visit 
Last week, my girlfriend came to visit — her first time in Nancy. I wanted to show her the city, and of course, a bit of culture had to be part of the plan. We’re both connected through dance: she comes from a ballet background, and I’m more of a hip-hop guy. So it was clear that an evening at the CCN – Ballet de Lorraine would be just the right choice.
I got us two tickets for the performance on November 5th 2025, at the National Opera of Nancy-Lorraine — third floor, far right, the kind of seats a student budget allows.
The evening’s repertoire included four performances: "Works" by Maud Le Pladec, "The Fugue" by Twyla Tharp, "TURNING BURNING" by Jan Martens, and "Synchronicité" by Maud Le Pladec. Each piece had its own distinct character.
The first, "Works", was almost hypnotic: sharp, angular movements set to electronic music. Next came "The Fugue", which took a more emotional turn — only breath and footsteps could be heard, no music, no distractions. In "TURNING BURNING", the energy exploded: spins, circles, repetitions — faster and faster, more and more intense. I almost got dizzy just watching. The evening closed with "Synchronicité": lights, beats, sneakers instead of pointe shoes — dynamic, modern, perfectly in sync. A piece that truly lived up to its name. Between the performances, we noticed some empty seats with a much better view. We quietly slipped into one of the vacant boxes — only to be politely but firmly reminded by the usher that switching seats wasn’t allowed. A guy next to us had the same idea and tried his luck. Since his French was way better than mine, he actually managed to keep the spot. But after the next piece, the usher came back and offered us the better seats. From then on: perfect view, perfect vibe. My takeaway from the evening: I know a bit about dance and have seen quite a few performances, but this one was special. Four pieces, four worlds — all connected by energy, precision, and boldness. A buildup that ended with a
bang. My girlfriend loved it too.
